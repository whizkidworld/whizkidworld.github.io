Privacy Policy — Amazing Arrows: Tap Puzzle

Effective date: 16 October 2026 · Last updated: 24 October 2026

App: Amazing Arrows: Tap Puzzle (com.whizkidworld.amazingarrows)

Amazing Arrows is a puzzle game published by Whizkid World LLP ("we", "us"), which is responsible for the information described here.

The short version

  • The game has no accounts of its own and no server of its own. We do not ask for your name, email address, phone number or location.
  • Your progress, badges, streak and settings are kept on your phone.
  • Ads are served by Google AdMob, which uses your device's advertising ID. In Europe, the UK and Switzerland you are asked first, and you can change your answer at any time.
  • The one purchase, Remove Ads, is handled entirely by Google Play Billing. We never see your payment details.
  • If the game crashes, a report goes to Google Firebase Crashlytics so we can fix it.
  • A short note of each level played goes to Google Analytics for Firebase, so we can tune difficulty. It never uses your advertising ID.
  • If you use Google Play Games, your badges, your leaderboard scores and a saved copy of your progress go to your own Google account.
  • If Google Play says a player is under 18, the game shows only non-personalised ads and sends no analytics.

Erase keeps only the record of your purchase (it belongs to your Google Play account), the age signal (see below), and a marker that tells your saved progress in Play Games which phone this is.

Backups

Android's Auto Backup is left on, so this data can follow you to a new phone. Android may copy it to your own Google Drive backup, under your Google account and Google's terms; it does not come to us. You can turn this off in your phone's settings under Google › Backup. The backup includes the record of your purchase, but it decides nothing on its own: each time the game starts with a connection, it checks your purchases with Google Play and removes anything Google Play no longer lists, such as a refunded purchase.

What leaves your device

Advertising — Google AdMob

The game shows two kinds of ad: a full-screen ad when you start a new level, at most once every five minutes of play (never on your first level of a session, and never before the daily puzzle or weekly challenge), and an optional video you can choose to watch for an extra strike or an extra hint. These are served by Google AdMob, Google's advertising service.

To do this, the Google Mobile Ads software in the game shares your device's advertising ID with Google, together with:

  • a coarse location derived from your IP address;
  • how you interact with the ads, for example that the app launched, that an ad was tapped, or that a video was watched;
  • diagnostic information about how the app and the ad software perform;
  • device identifiers: the advertising ID and Android's app set ID.

Google collects and uses these to choose which ads to show you, to measure them and to detect fraud. In the Play Store's Data safety terms, that is collected, and shared with a third party, for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention. It is encrypted in transit. We never receive it; what reaches us is an anonymous count of ads shown.

We ask AdMob to limit ads to a PG rating (G for a player Google Play says is under 13), and ads are muted. Google explains what it collects here: How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services.

Your choice about ads

If you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, you are shown a consent form before any ad is requested, using Google's User Messaging Platform. If you decline, ads may still be shown, but only in Google's limited form, which is not personalised and does not use your device's storage. If the question cannot be answered (for example, no connection on the first launch), no ads are requested at all until it can be, and every reward a video would unlock is granted anyway.

You can reopen that form and change your answer at any time from Player › Privacy › Ad privacy choices, which appears wherever the law requires the choice. Everywhere, you can reset or delete your advertising ID in your phone's settings under Privacy › Ads.

Crash reports — Google Firebase Crashlytics

When the game crashes, or hits an error it recovers from, a report goes to Google Firebase Crashlytics so the fault can be found and fixed. A report contains the error and which part of the code failed; device and system information (model, manufacturer, Android version, memory and storage, and whether the device was rooted); the app version and how long it had been running; and an installation identifier generated by Firebase, so repeated crashes from one install can be grouped. It is not your advertising ID and is not linked to your identity. Firebase also reports basic app session events (that the game was opened, and roughly for how long), which is how a crash is tied to a run.

Your progress, badges, purchase and settings are never attached to these reports, and they are not used for advertising. In the Play Store's Data safety terms this is Crash logs and Diagnostics under App info and performance, and Device or other IDs, collected for app functionality.

Crashlytics deletes a report after 90 days. Because a report carries no name, email or account, we cannot find your reports from a message, so we cannot delete particular reports on request; clearing the game's data or uninstalling starts a new installation identifier. Google processes these on our behalf: Firebase privacy and security. Crash reporting is off in the development builds we use while making the game.

Analytics — Google Analytics for Firebase

To learn how the game is really played (which levels are too hard, where players stop), the game sends a short note to Google Analytics for Firebase when:

  • a level starts (your first tap): the mode, the level number, the strike setting, and whether it was a resumed level;
  • a level is won: the same, plus the time taken, strikes, hints used, stars, and extra strikes from videos;
  • a level is lost or given up, or left unfinished: the mode, the level number, the time, strikes and hints;
  • the daily puzzle or weekly challenge is finished: the time, strikes, and whether it was your best this week.

Firebase adds what it records for every app that uses it: that the app was opened or updated, how long it was in use, a purchase made through Google Play (the item and its price), the device model, Android version, language, app version, and an approximate country from your IP address. Because the analytics is connected to our AdMob account, Firebase also records each ad shown and what it earned. Each installation gets an app-instance identifier, generated by Firebase, so one installation's levels can be counted together. The advertising ID is never collected for analytics. Analytics is connected to AdMob only to measure what ads earn; it is not used to choose which ads you see, and the data reaches us only as totals and charts.

In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland the app-instance identifier is not stored, so your levels are not linked to one another. Everywhere else it is. In the Play Store's Data safety terms this is App interactions under App activity, Purchase history under Financial info, and Device or other IDs, collected for analytics and not shared. Google keeps it for 14 months. Analytics is on only in the version you install from Google Play, and never for a player Google Play says is under 18. Erase all progress also resets the app-instance identifier.

Purchases — Google Play Billing

The game offers one purchase, Remove Ads, bought once and kept. It switches off the full-screen ads between levels; the optional videos for an extra strike or hint stay, because removing them would take away something you might want. Every transaction is handled by Google Play Billing: we never see your card, your billing address or your Google account. Google tells the game only whether the purchase succeeded.

Ratings — Google Play

Now and then, after a good win and never after a loss, the game may ask Google Play to show its own rating window, and Player › Help › Rate the app opens the game's Play Store page. A rating goes to Google and is published under Google's terms; the game sends nothing for it and is not told whether you rated. You can stop the game asking in Player › Help.

Age signals — Google Play

Where the law requires it, Google Play can tell the game a player's age range (never a birthdate) through its age signals service. The game asks Google Play on your phone when it starts and when you return to it, and keeps the answer on your phone (and in your own Android backup); it is never sent to us. Until Google Play has answered once, the game shows only the non-personalised ads it would show a player under 18. If Google Play says a player is under 18, the game shows only non-personalised ads suitable for a family audience and sends no analytics. If it says a parent has not yet approved this version for a supervised account, the game waits for that approval before it can be played. Elsewhere Google Play gives no age and nothing changes. The game never asks your age itself.

Achievements, leaderboard and saved progress — Google Play Games

Google Play Games is optional; everything in the game works without it. If you have a Play Games profile and have allowed it, Play Games signs you in automatically when the game starts; you can turn that off in the Google Play Games app, or sign in later from the game's Player tab. While you are signed in:

  • Achievements. Each badge you earn is also unlocked in Play Games and shown on your Play Games profile. Badges earned before you signed in are sent when you do.
  • Leaderboards. Three scores are posted to Play Games leaderboards: your total score (the sum of your best score on every level), your best score on today's daily puzzle and your best score on this week's challenge. Other players can see them with your Play Games name and picture, according to your Play Games profile settings; a private profile keeps them hidden. A level won with the help of a video is not posted.
  • Saved progress. A copy of your level results (Custom bests included), badges, the tips you have seen, daily and weekly results, play statistics, your name and the settings listed above is kept in Play Games' saved games under your own Google account, and merged with the copy on any other phone you sign in on. It never holds a level in progress, your purchase, the reminder, ad timing or your age signal.

All of this is kept by Google under your Google account and Google's terms; none of it comes to us. To delete it, use the Google Play Games app's settings to delete your Play Games data for this game or your whole profile. Erase all progress, while signed in, also empties the saved copy.

Notifications

If you turn on the daily reminder (the game offers it once, after your first daily puzzle, and you can change it in Player › Settings), the game schedules a local notification on your phone at the time you choose, and only when that day's puzzle is unplayed. If you allow Alarms & reminders for the game in Android's settings (it asks once, when you turn the reminder on), it comes at exactly that time; otherwise Android may send it up to an hour later. It is created and shown by your phone; nothing is sent to us or anyone else. Turning the reminder off cancels it.

Children

The game is not directed at children, and its Play Store audience is 16 and over. We do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone: we have no accounts and receive nothing that identifies you. Where Google Play's age signals say a player is under 18, the game treats them as described above.

Deleting your data

What the game keeps on your phone you can erase yourself, as described above. Crash reports and analytics carry no name or account, so we cannot pick out yours, and they are deleted automatically after 90 days and 14 months. Ad data is held by Google; use your phone's ad settings and the links above. Step by step: games.whizkidworld.in/amazing-arrows/delete-data/.
